One thing I learned in cell biology which was very interesting is the dynamics of plasma membrane.
When seen as a part of the cell, it appears to be a rigid wall. But it is not. It is a moving sea. And this was proved by scientists using a genius technique called FRAP (Fluorescence Recovery After Photobleaching).
The plasma membrane is uniformly labeled with fluorescence molecules (like Green Fluorescent Protein, GFP). This forms the basis of FRAP.
In FRAP, an initial baseline image is captured using low-intensity light. Then, high-intensity, focused laser beam is targeted at a very small region of interest. This intense light damages (photobleaches) the fluorescence capability of that particular region of the membrane, making it look dark.
Using low-intensity light again, the microscope records the bleached area over time. It can be noticed that, the neighboring unbleached fluorescent molecules randomly diffuse into the dark spot and the bleached molecules migrate out. Hence the fluorescence intensity of that region gradually increases.
This proves the Fluid Mosaic Model of plasma membrane by showing how lipids and the membrane proteins drift laterally across the cell membrane.
I never expected any kind of lateral movement within the plasma membrane. So I see this as another marvel of life.
